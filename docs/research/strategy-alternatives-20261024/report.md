# Fix execution before tuning LARP's strategy

As built at commit `59c878c0` (branch `codex/system-testing-20261012`, Oct 24), LARP's planned S1 (TREND_GUARDED_VOL) does not follow trends. The production service passes no price series, so the trend guard never leaves its UP start state. At 200 USDT, its 95%→60% cut (about 14 USDT per name at 1/5 weight) is also smaller than the 18.64 USDT minimum trade, so it could not trade even with the series wired. What actually runs is a static book of five names at about 95% gross. It has a Class 2 entry block, Class 1 exits, a 3%/8%/12% loss ladder whose 8% buy stop never resets, and a terminal flatten before Nov 11 15:00 UTC. The EQUAL_WEIGHT fallback holds four names at 20% (80% gross) and builds one swap per session. S2, the weekend test, is a rehearsal-only book of five names at 19% each that filled 1 of 4 live buys. No part of S1 has out-of-sample evidence that it adds profit. The team's own EXP-008 found that inverse-σ weighting and the trend guard each cost roughly a quarter to a third of a percentage point (pp) per 10 sessions against equal weight, and the external literature agrees. A 10-session window's expected return is a few tenths of a percent against a standard deviation of 4–6%, so the profit levers that matter are the ones that get the book built and keep it invested. The best-supported changes need only a new profile: execution settings that let fills complete, equal 1/5 weights (already used in every S1 rehearsal), formally retiring the guard for v1.0 rather than wiring it, and the decided PF-04 calendar. Sizing the gas seed needs no code, but its value (anywhere from 0 to about 1 pp) depends on the unresolved question of whether BNB counts in equity. Every code-level option offers at most about ±0.1 pp, or reduces only drawdown, and none justifies requalification before final activation on Oct 27–29.

## As built, S1 is a static 95% book with a loss ladder

**What the agent can run.** The code knows four baselines, but `validate_live` admits only `EQUAL_WEIGHT` and `TREND_GUARDED_VOL` for a LIVE profile ([profiles.py:109-116](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/strategy/profiles.py#L109-L116)). On 2026-10-21 the team chose "TREND_GUARDED_VOL / S1, up to 95% gross" for the final, keeping SP-D2's measured EQUAL_WEIGHT rehearsal as the fallback if S1 fails qualification. This was a delegated team POLICY choice, not an organiser rule ([IDEA-084:40-58](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/IDEA-084.md#L40-L58)). The design note frames S1 honestly: "The trend guard and the limiter are there to cut drawdown in a falling market… Neither is a source of edge" ([IDEA-075:8](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/IDEA-075.md#L8)). The choice is still open. After the weekend test, Yao is due to pick "S1 or another version" on Monday Oct 26, and S2 is never used for the final ([IDEA-089:85-89](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/IDEA-089.md#L85-L89)).

**How S1 is designed.** S1's target gross is the smaller of a trend level and a limiter level, and it is applied to frozen weights. Each weight is proportional to 1/σ of 60 session log returns, capped at 30% ([s1.py:269-330](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/strategy/s1.py#L269-L330)). The trend guard compares an equal-weight basket index with its 5-session mean. Above +1% it moves to UP (95%), below −1% to DOWN (60%), and between the two it keeps its state ([s1.py:142-158](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/strategy/s1.py#L142-L158)). The limiter can only reduce exposure. A Class 2 red flag on two or more names gives CAUTION (60%), and a trading suspension gives SEVERE (30%) ([s1.py:180-195](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/strategy/s1.py#L180-L195)). The loss ladder has three rungs, all hard-coded:

- `daily_halt`: blocks buys for the rest of the UTC day after a 3% fall from the day's anchor.
- `run_halt`: blocks buys after an 8% fall from peak.
- `deep_cut`: trims gross to 40% after a 12% fall from peak.

The ladder is set in [accounting.py:26-38](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/risk/accounting.py#L26-L38). Discretionary trades run only from 30 minutes after the open to 15 minutes before the close, with 3 attempts per leg per session ([execution_bounds.py:99-105](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/strategy/execution_bounds.py#L99-L105)).

**What runs instead.** The production service builds its `ContextBuilder` without a `series` argument ([service.py:330-333](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/runtime/service.py#L330-L333)). `evaluate_trend` then returns `TREND_INPUT_UNAVAILABLE` and keeps the start state ([s1.py:127-130](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/strategy/s1.py#L127-L130)). The live limiter producer emits only Class 2 filings and lists `TRADE_RESTRICTION`, `TRADING_SUSPENSION` and `SCHEDULED_EVENT` as unsupported, so SEVERE can never fire ([exposure.py:50-56](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/integration/exposure.py#L50-L56)).

The minimum trade disables the remaining cuts. The policy sells toward a lower level only when a name's gap is at least the minimum trade ([policy.py:186-215](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/strategy/policy.py#L186-L215)), and every committed profile sets that minimum to 18.637 USDT. The figure comes from MSFTB's 17 bp off-session spread ([profiles.py:147-165](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/strategy/profiles.py#L147-L165)). A CAUTION or DOWN cut from 95% to 60% at a 1/5 weight is 0.35 × 0.2 × 199.8 ≈ **14.0 USDT per name, below the minimum, so nothing trades**. Only names weighted at 0.267 or more could be trimmed. Under inverse-σ weights, a DOWN signal would therefore trim only the heaviest name, which is also the lowest-volatility one: the opposite of the intended de-risking.

The one rung that acts at full size is the 12% deep cut. It is a mandatory trim that "always trims at its own limit" ([planner.py:194-201](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/risk/planner.py#L194-L201)). The team decided (PF-02) that the 8% buy stop clears once drawdown is below 5% ([IDEA-084:60-68](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/IDEA-084.md#L60-L68)). The code still latches it for the whole run with `|=` ([accounting.py:33](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/risk/accounting.py#L33)).

## The fallback and the weekend test differ in build speed, hours and readiness

**EQUAL_WEIGHT is a slower, smaller version of the same idea.** It holds four names at 20% each, buying the cheapest measured round trip first and skipping red-flagged names. It makes new buys only while gross is below 75%. It trims a name above 25% back to 20% and gross above 90% back to 80%, and never sells on drift ([IDEA-037:10-23](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/IDEA-037.md#L10-L23)). Its live allocations are pinned in code ([profiles.py:119-137](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/strategy/profiles.py#L119-L137)). It allows one discretionary swap per calendar session ([planner.py:378-380](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/risk/planner.py#L378-L380)), so **the book takes four sessions to build**, against one for S1 ([IDEA-049-B:90](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/IDEA-049-B.md#L90)). Over the window, the average name is held about 1.5 sessions less.

EW also has no NYSE-hours execution bounds ([planner.py:397-406](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/risk/planner.py#L397-L406)). With the midnight generator windows used by every committed FINAL candidate (all EW), it could start buying at Oct 29 00:00 UTC. That is US after-hours, just after the Microsoft and Alphabet earnings releases.

The irony is that **EW is the only strategy with a built FINAL profile**, while S1 is the strategy actually chosen.

**S2 was an execution test, not a strategy.** It is "a REHEARSAL-only test profile for the dev wallet, not a contest strategy". It runs S1's five names at 19% each, with trend and limiter fixed at UP/NORMAL, trading whenever the kit's `openState` is true, with 5 attempts per leg, from Sat Oct 24 01:00Z to Sun Oct 25 15:00Z ([IDEA-089:42-60](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/IDEA-089.md#L42-L60)). Validation refuses its 24/7 hours mode outside a dev REHEARSAL ([execution_bounds.py:44-51](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/strategy/execution_bounds.py#L44-L51)).

The live result is the most important fact in this report. **Of four signed BUY intents, one completed and three aborted** with "encoded swap economics mismatch/expired", and two approvals confirmed with no swap behind them ([IDEA-090:169-227](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/IDEA-090.md#L169-L227)). The root cause (S2-F6) was that the post-approval requote had to return at least the planning quote's minimum at the same 8 bp slippage. The fixes for S2-F2 to S2-F6 are in the source but have not been run live ([IDEA-090:118-157](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/IDEA-090.md#L118-L157); [INCOMPLETE_WORK.md:107](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/INCOMPLETE_WORK.md#L107)). At this commit there is no live sell or flatten evidence at all. Sunday's flatten falls after it.

**Costs are low and well measured. Execution reliability is not.** Measured quote spreads (round trip, worse-of-regime medians) are 2–5 bp for GOOGLB, NVDAB, SKHYB and TSLAB and 17 bp for MSFTB. US-session medians are 0.7–7 bp ([QUOTE_COST_REVIEW_120:31-50](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/build/QUOTE_COST_REVIEW_120_20261021.md#L31-L50)). The 13–56 bp figures that still circulate are older Roll estimates from trade prints, not quotes, and have been superseded ([IDEA-012:12-20](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/IDEA-012.md#L12-L20)).

The first live fill used **0.0000242 BNB per approve-plus-swap**, against a reservation of 0.000384 BNB ([IDEA-089:369-384](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/IDEA-089.md#L369-L384)). BscScan showed 0.05 gwei on Oct 24 ([BscScan](https://bscscan.com/gastracker)). At about 722 USDT per BNB, gas is about 0.0175 USDT per leg, which is 9 bp of a 19 USDT trade or 4.6 bp of a 38 USDT trade. Building and liquidating the whole book therefore costs roughly 0.1–0.15% of equity. That is far below the 25 bp per side that EXP-008 assumed.

An external volatility model explains why 8 bp is fragile. With NVDA's 37.6% 30-day implied volatility ([thetrading.tools](https://www.thetrading.tools/nvda-options)) and TSLA's 51.1% for early November ([MarketChameleon](https://marketchameleon.com/Overview/TSLA/OptionChain/)), a one-sided adverse move beyond 8 bp is expected about 17–25% of the time over 30–60 s for NVDA and 24–31% for TSLA, from noise alone. The team's own 8 bp is the worst-name p90 of *15-second* requote drift ([profiles.py:245](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/strategy/profiles.py#L245)). Any plan-to-requote gap with an approval confirmation inside it is longer than 15 seconds. The live 75% abort rate is far above noise, which fits the approval-inside-the-quote-window mechanism that S2-F6 found. A signed RFQ price cannot change during settlement ([Spark](https://www.spark.money/glossary/request-for-quote)), so a wider minimum-output tolerance mainly widens the drift check rather than worsening fills.

## Every S1 overlay costs return and buys only drawdown

**EXP-008 compared S1 with EW, and EW won on profit.** It ran on 1,954 rolling 10-session windows of split-adjusted daily closes, 2019–2026, using underlying-stock proxies. **S1 averaged +0.34% (SD 4.1%) against EW's +0.92% (SD 5.2%)**, a gap of −0.58 pp with a 90% CI of −0.77 to −0.41. S1 was ahead in only 36% of windows and behind in every calendar year. In exchange it cut the chance of an 8% drawdown from 9.7% to 3.5% ([EXP-008:84-95](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/EXP-008.md#L84-L95)).

The decomposition puts −0.24 pp on the weights and name set, *after* S1's 15 pp of extra gross exposure, and −0.33 pp on the trend guard. Inverse-σ moved weight from the highest-return names (NVDAB, TSLAB, SKHYB) to the lowest (GOOGLB, MSFTB). The guard sat in DOWN on 37% of sessions and flipped about 35 times a year: "the guard mostly whipsaws" ([EXP-008:97-105](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/EXP-008.md#L97-L105)).

The team kept S1 anyway, recording that this is "a judgement against the pre-registered outcome". The record also notes three weaknesses of the experiment: the simulator predates the criteria commit by minutes, the Yahoo data snapshot is not reproducible from Git ([EXP-008:112-126](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/EXP-008.md#L112-L126)), and plan D-09 forbids using Yahoo as a data source. The dedicated guard test, EXP-007, has never been run. Its own pre-registered rule was that a NOT_SUPPORTED result fixes the trend level at 95% ([EXP-007:6-20](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/EXP-007.md#L6-L20)).

**External evidence points the same way on all three overlays.**

- *Weighting.* Across seven datasets, none of 14 optimised allocation rules consistently beat 1/N on Sharpe ratio or certainty-equivalent return ([DeMiguel, Garlappi & Uppal 2009](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v22y2009i5p1915-1953.html)). The low-turnover volatility-timing rules that do beat 1/N win on Sharpe, not raw return ([Kirby & Ostdiek 2012](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/its-all-in-the-timing-simple-active-portfolio-strategies-that-outperform-naive-diversification/05D18E04B226C6E9B8441482CC92F943)). For an unlevered book scored on return, inverse-σ cannot raise expected return. It moves weight from high-volatility names to low-volatility ones and gains only lower variance.
- *Volatility management.* In real time it beat unmanaged portfolios on Sharpe in **53 of 103 equity strategies against 50**, and lowered certainty-equivalent return in 72 of 103. The literature's gains depend on levering up in calm periods, which LARP cannot do ([Cederburg et al. 2020](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)). For US equities sorted by last month's volatility, "the mean return shows no clear pattern" ([Harvey et al. 2018](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)). Cutting exposure in volatile periods therefore cuts expected return roughly one for one.
- *Stops and trend filters.* Under a random walk, stop-loss rules always lower expected return ([Kaminski & Lo 2014](https://swopec.hhs.se/sifrwp/abs/sifrwp0063.htm)). Tight stops on individual US stocks lose, while wide 10-day stops roughly match buy-and-hold and cut maximum drawdown ([Lo & Remorov via CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-premium/stop-losses-on-stock-positions-in-depth)). The best daily technical rule earned a statistically insignificant 8.63% a year *before costs* out of sample in 1987–96 ([farmdoc on Sullivan–Timmermann–White](https://farmdoc.illinois.edu/assets/meetings/nccc134/conf_2004/pdf/confp23-04.pdf)). Individual US stocks still show significantly negative daily autocorrelation ([Lewellen](https://faculty.tuck.dartmouth.edu/images/uploads/faculty/jonathan-lewellen/Autocorrelations.pdf)), so a 5-day ±1% band sells into dips that partly reverse. The guard's ±1% hysteresis implies a path loss of roughly 2% × 35% ≈ 0.7% of equity per full whipsaw, which is consistent with EXP-008's cost and much larger than its fees.

**No timing signal clears costs over this window.**

- *Reversal and momentum.* Short-term reversal is largely consumed by costs ([de Groot, Huij & Zhou](https://efmaefm.org/0EFMAMEETINGS/EFMA%20ANNUAL%20MEETINGS/2011-Braga/papers/0259_update.pdf)), and it flips to short-term momentum in the most liquid, most covered stocks ([Medhat & Schmeling](https://repec.cepr.org/repec/cpr/ceprdp/DP15857.pdf)). Neither tilt has a reliable sign for these five names.
- *FOMC.* No FOMC decision falls inside the window: the Oct 27–28 meeting precedes it and the next is Dec 8–9 ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fomccalendars.htm)). The pre-FOMC drift "essentially disappeared after 2015" anyway ([Kurov et al.](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v40y2021ics1544612320315956.html)).
- *Earnings.* SK hynix, Microsoft and Alphabet all report on Oct 28, before the window ([SK hynix](https://news.skhynix.com/en/?p=13734); [Microsoft](https://news.microsoft.com/source/2026/10/07/microsoft-announces-quarterly-earnings-release-date-69/); [PrimeXBT on Alphabet](https://primexbt.com/for-traders/alphabet-earnings-date/)). NVIDIA is expected around Nov 18, after it ([days.to](https://days.to/nvidia-earnings)). Large-cap post-earnings drift has been absent since 2006 ([Martineau 2022](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/martineau2021rest.pdf)).
- *News signals from LLMs.* The documented edge lasts one to two days, is concentrated in small stocks and negative news, becomes unprofitable at 20 bp round trip, and its Sharpe ratio fell from 6.54 to 1.22 by 2024 ([Lopez-Lira & Tang](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07619v6)).
- *Off-hours token prices.* These moves looked like partial price discovery, not noise to fade, in the one weekend studied ([CoinPaprika](https://coinpaprika.com/education/tokenized-stocks-price-tracking/)). Weekend equity perps had "effectively no predictive value" ([Blockworks Research](https://blockworks-research.beehiiv.com/p/when-markets-sleep)).

**The case for S1 deserves a fair hearing.** S1 did halve the frequency of 8% drawdowns. On the small token-candle sample (4 non-overlapping windows), static S1 led EW by +0.48 pp and only the guard cost return (−0.50 pp) ([EXP-008:97-105](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/EXP-008.md#L97-L105)). The 2019–2026 proxy sample is selection-biased toward the high-volatility winners the organisers listed. Broad samples show high-beta assets earning *lower* alphas ([Frazzini & Pedersen 2014](https://research-api.cbs.dk/ws/portalfiles/portal/60082899/lasse_heje_pedersen_et_al_betting_against_beta_publishersversion.pdf)), so EW's measured margin over inverse-σ overstates the structural gap. The defensible claim is narrower: inverse-σ cannot raise expected return without leverage, and the guard pays an insurance premium that no evidence says is underpriced.

Above all, the stakes are small. IDEA-075's synthetic estimate for 90–100% gross was a mean of **+0.16 to +0.19% per 10 sessions with an SD of 5.2–5.8%**, and the note concedes that S1 "cannot be shown before the run to make money" ([IDEA-075:164-170](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/ledger/IDEA-075.md#L164-L170)).

## Ranked alternatives put execution and equal weights first, and code last

**How the list is ranked.** The ranking weighs evidence strength and expected effect against what each change costs to qualify before the Oct 27–29 freeze. A profile-only change still produces a new profile hash. **Rank 7 options are not worth the risk before the freeze.**

- *PF-02 reset.* For a static book it barely matters: gross stays near 95% as prices fall, so top-ups rarely trigger, and the deep-cut latch stays on regardless. Its value is consistency with the declared PF-02 decision, which a deviation row can also provide.
- *CPPI cushion.* Exposure proportional to the cushion above a floor set relative to peak equity ([Grossman & Zhou 1993](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/mathfi/v3y1993i3p241-276.html)) is the efficient way to buy drawdown control, at a small negative expected-return cost.
- *100% gross.* This is a code constant. In EXP-008's bull sample, the extra 5 pp is worth about 0.06 pp.
- *Flatten after Nov 11 15:00 UTC.* This would recover about two sessions of exposure, but it is a team position encoded as a schema ceiling ([schema.py:135-142](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/larp/strategy/schema.py#L135-L142)). It also bets on the unresolved exact end instant (Q-06) and on how open positions are scored (Q-39).

## Rank math rewards staying invested; drawdown is a secondary constraint

**Rules checked.** The prize goes to **the top 3 of fewer than 25 teams**. There is no live leaderboard, the proposed score is the percentage change in wallet equity, and lower maximum drawdown breaks ties ([source_page.md:34-38](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/official/source_page.md#L34-L38); [source_page.md:84](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/official/source_page.md#L84); [source_page.md:104-106](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/official/source_page.md#L104-L106)). The organisers have also said they look at completed-trade PnL, drawdown, logs and the code repo "all together" (F-115). They have said orders "can be placed 24/7" (F-119), so the NYSE-hours limit is team POLICY. Nothing in the rules caps gross exposure, weighting or trade count. The constraints are long-only bStocks against USDT, no human action and a frozen build (F-117) ([SKILL.md F-117](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/skill/SKILL.md#L203); [SKILL.md F-119](https://github.com/binance-hackathon-glasswing/binance-hackathon/blob/59c878c03fb47c05e7a76c327057f5568ab21def/docs/skill/SKILL.md#L207)).

**What tournament theory says.** When fewer than half the contestants win, a contestant should choose a riskier distribution that is less correlated with rivals ([Gaba, Tsetlin & Winkler 2004](https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/pdf/10.1287/opre.1030.0098)). Under convex payoffs, it is often optimal to put everything in the most promising alternative ([Schwarz & Severinov 2010](https://ideas.repec.org/a/ucp/jlabec/v28y2010i4p893-922.html)). A half-invested basket sits in the middle of the field's distribution, the worst place for a top-3 payoff. That argues for keeping S1's 95% over EW's 80%, not the reverse.

**What the closest real contests showed.** The nearest live analogue ran for two weeks on overlapping US tech names, and **only 6 of 32 model-contest sessions were profitable**. The losses were blamed on overtrading and sizing errors ([BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/5BSX_Z0BaoGGrU-ID27J)). Bias-controlled benchmarks find LLM strategies "too cautious when risk is rewarded" ([FINSABER](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.07078v5)). A low-turnover, invested book is therefore well placed to beat the median rival. A top-3 finish still needs the market's common move to go the right way.

**The concentration lever is limited.** The only concentration available by profile is a tilt toward the highest-volatility names, capped at 30% each. Its expected-return sign is unknown, it deepens drawdowns, and the field it would need to beat cannot be observed. It is a decision about variance for the team to make, not a profit improvement. The empirical risk-shifting literature is itself contested ([Busse 2001](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v36y2001i01p53-73_00.html)).

**Drawdown matters less than it looks.** An exact tie in percentage return is improbable unless two teams never trade. Drawdown therefore counts mainly through the F-115 holistic review, and there the 12% mandatory deep cut is the one control that acts at full size.

**Calendar notes.** The scored calendar supports holding rather than timing. The first S1 buy at 14:00 UTC on Oct 29 lands after the GDP/PCE release ([BEA](https://www.bea.gov/index.php/news/schedule/full)) and in the session that reacts to Microsoft's and Alphabet's earnings. The turn-of-month days and the Nov 3 election fall early in the window, and the evidence that these seasonals persist is contested ([ETF Trends](https://www.etftrends.com/etf-strategist-channel/turn-month-effect/); [Atlanta Fed](https://fedinprint.org/item/fedawp/13394/original)). Read cautiously, they argue against de-risking then, not for any rule. The US session moves an hour later in UTC after Nov 1 ([Time Out](https://www.timeout.com/usa/news/when-do-the-clocks-go-back-in-2026-heres-when-daylight-saving-time-officially-ends-100626)). PF-04's times already reflect the shift, but the notes do not establish that the `NYSE_SESSION` bounds are computed in New York time, so that is worth one test before the freeze.

## Conclusion

Monday's question is less "S1 or EW?" than it appears. As built, both are nearly static long books. What separates the plausible outcomes is whether the book gets built and flattened reliably, what the weighting gives up, and accounting choices (the gas seed, the windows) that are worth as much as any strategy setting. The better question is "which static book, executed how?" The evidence-backed answer is S1's code running equal 1/5 weights at 95%, with the guard formally retired for v1.0, a deliberately wider slippage limit and the maximum attempts. That configuration is closest to what the team has already rehearsed, so it adds the least qualification risk. EQUAL_WEIGHT remains a sound fallback, slightly lower in expected exposure.

Two items have more expected value than any further backtest: one live buy-and-sell cycle with the merged S2 fixes before the freeze, and an organiser answer to Q-39 on BNB valuation and completed trades. Both need nothing from the strategy code and both arrive before the build is frozen. A strategy decided without them is optimising tenths of a point while points sit unaddressed.
